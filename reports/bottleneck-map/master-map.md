- Applications: GaN (gallium nitride) → EV power chips, 5G RF, data-center power conversion; GaAs (gallium arsenide) → RF front-end, optical devices

**Key difference between the U.S. and Japan cases**:
- For the U.S.: the ban is suspended through 2026-11-27, managed via licenses, with exports still occurring
- **For Japan: controls have been in effect since January 2026, with actual exports at zero in January–February and no exemption arrangement**; Japan is a major global GaN/GaAs semiconductor manufacturing base (Sumitomo Electric RF chips, Mitsubishi Electric, Murata power devices)

**Bottleneck determination (Japan's supply chain)**:
- Supply concentration 🔴 (99% China, zero imports into Japan)
- Expansion cycle 🔴 (gallium is a byproduct of aluminum smelting; new Western sources take 5–10 years)
- Substitutability 🟡 (GaN can be partially substituted by SiC, but switching costs are high; there is currently no substitute for GaAs in RF)
= **B+ tier** (reason for the upgrade: the cutoff to Japan is now measured, not just a forward-looking risk; but Japanese manufacturers claim sufficient inventory, so this has not yet become an acute crisis); if the ban against the U.S. is reinstated after 2026-11-27 → A-tier
